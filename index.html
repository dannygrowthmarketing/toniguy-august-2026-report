In twenty months on record, only two months have cleared ₹2.9 Cr — May 2026 and August 2026. Both fall inside these six months. The best month before the rebuild was ₹2.75 Cr.

What six months made, in reality

The swing

+₹1.03 Cr

February 2026 was the worst month in the twenty-month record at ₹1.91 Cr. August is ₹2.94 Cr. A 54% monthly swing in six months.

Annual run-rate

₹28.1 → ₹32.2 Cr

Six-month revenue annualised. A ₹4.12 Cr improvement in what the estate runs at.

Bills added

+10,659

Against the same six months last year. 337 bills a day in August alone.

Demand built

2.6× at 1.7× cost

1,18,253 MQLs against 44,625 last year, on 1.74× the media spend.

Two comparisons. Both point the same way.

Against August 2025 — strict like-for-like — revenue is up 7.0%, bills 13.4%, Google leads 122.6%.

Against February 2026 — the month the work started — revenue is up 54.1%, bills 29.4%, average bill 19.2%.

August set all-time highs for impressions, clicks and MQLs. It was the second-best month on record for both revenue and bills, behind May 2026.

The honest counter: blended revenue per marketing rupee fell from 96.5× to 63.5×, and cost per bill rose from ₹29 to ₹42. Scale always costs efficiency. The fair test for growth spend is the incremental one above — 19.1× — and the counterfactual is not a flat business. It was falling. February 2026 was the worst month on record.

Three things happened at once. Only one is a problem.

One — two campaigns overspent their worth. Demand Gen and Koramangala 4th PMAX took ₹2,64,528 between them. At account-average efficiency that output costs ₹60,073. ₹2,04,455 is recoverable — 63% of the month's entire Google increase.

Two — Meta genuinely scaled. ₹1.13 L to ₹2.73 L, with lead cost down 37%. That is not waste. That is buying more of something that got cheaper.

Three — the month's conversions are concentrated in one week. That needs explaining before 22,532 is treated as settled.

Flagged before
anyone else finds it

From 6 to 12 August the account reported 19.3% conversion rates. Across the other twenty-four days it averaged 1.7%. That is not a normal distribution.

Two explanations fit. A real seven-day surge in store-visit response — click-through rate rose with it, which supports this. Or a change in how a conversion action was counted during that window.

Those seven days carry most of the month's 22,532 conversions. If it is real, find what ran that week and run it again. If it is a counting change, August's cost per MQL is understated. Either way it is one hour in the conversions report, and it should happen before September's budget is set.

The one number
to watch

Average bill is ₹2,813. Up 6.0% on July. Up 19.2% since February. Still 5.7% below August 2025's ₹2,983.

At August's volume, closing that gap is worth ₹17.78 L a month — more than the entire marketing budget.

Product is 5.4% of sales. Packages are 28.1%. Both are salon-floor levers. Neither has moved with traffic.
